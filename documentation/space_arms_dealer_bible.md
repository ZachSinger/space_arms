# Space Arms Dealer Simulation - Master Technical & Design Blueprint (Updated Steps 1-68)

## Current Stretch Goals List (Post-MVP)
* **Active Theft / Security Mini-Game:** Physical interception of shoplifters on the canvas.
* **The Military Faction:** A fourth customer demographic with unique bulk/high-tier purchasing habits.
* **Faction-Specific Rewards:** Tangible bonuses or unlocks for maximizing reputation with specific groups.
* **Alternative Bankruptcy / Bailout Systems:** Debt spirals or faction loans to prevent a soft-lock game over.
* **Advanced Supplier Discovery System:** Actively researching/discovering new suppliers beyond the default list.

---

## Part 1: Core Architecture & Time
* **Step 1: Platform & Tech Stack:** PC Desktop (Electron using Vite, vanilla HTML/CSS/JS frontend, and Phaser 3 canvas). The UI and Canvas are strictly decoupled.
* **Step 2: Time Management:** Real-time simulation using Galactic Time Code (GTC) with a "Manual Toggle". Shifts reach soft close after 12 GTC hours at a **Static Speed**, or 7 minutes of unpaused play; time pauses entirely when closed, allowing infinite downtime for management.
* **Pause Menu:** P, Esc, or the HUD Pause button freezes the shop, physics, animations,
  shelf pulses, sound, and timers. Resume, Save Game, Load Game, Settings, Exit to Main
  Menu, and Exit to Desktop are available. Saving remains between shifts only; desktop
  exit is disabled in a browser. Loading and exiting confirm unsaved progress will be lost.
  Settings can be used without resuming. Both cursor modes use custom artwork: hardware
  is system-drawn, software is game-drawn; the choice is saved with Apply or Accept.
* **Keyboard & Mouse Controls:** Settings offers up to two keys or mouse buttons for each
  listed game action. Changes preview immediately; Apply saves a new cancel point, Accept
  saves and closes, and Cancel restores the last applied settings. Conflicting controls are
  rejected, defaults can be restored, and every action keeps at least one control. Escape
  still closes menus. The handbook and gameplay prompts show the current bindings.
* **Step 3: The MVP Bridge:** Powered by a Master State (single source of truth) and an Event Bus to handle communication between the "dumb" UI and the Canvas.
* **Player Dash & Stamina:** Hold Shift while moving with WASD/arrows to dash at 280 pixels per second instead of walking at 160. Only the player has this ability; employees are unchanged. The HUD shows a stamina bar below the status bar. Starting balance: 100 stamina, 25 used per second of dashing, and 25 recovered per second while walking or resting after a 0.75-second delay. At zero stamina the player walks until stamina recovers to 25, preventing rapid speed changes at an empty bar. Shift pause freezes stamina along with movement; management windows allow recovery while the player rests. Stamina starts full when a game is loaded and is not saved.
* **Controller Support:** Any standard controller (Xbox layout names) can play the whole game; prompts switch to controller buttons as soon as it's used and back to keys on any real key press or mouse use. These are the default button assignments. In the shop: left stick or D-pad walks, B or LT dashes, holding RT while facing a shelf or register opens its color window, holding the left stick in lights up all trash on the floor, A interacts, X picks up crates, Y puts crates down, clicking the right stick moves the green crate arrow one crate down, B leaves the register, Back/View opens the Manager's Tablet, Start opens the top bar (pause, start shift, end day, save, close store, main menu), and LB/RB zoom while on the sales floor. In any window, the D-pad or left stick moves a yellow highlight; A clicks, X acts as right-click, B goes back (from the register screen it leaves the register), LB/RB switch tablet tabs, and the right stick scrolls. On a shelf, Y chooses which carried crate stocks next. When moving a fixture, the right stick moves it on an 8-pixel grid while the left stick or D-pad walks, Y turns it, A places it, and X cancels. In Settings under Controller, players can change which actions use the face, bumper, menu, stick-click, and RT buttons; assigning a button already in use switches the two actions. LT always dashes. The D-pad and analog stick directions keep their existing jobs. Prompts and help show the current button assignment.

* **Controller menu interactions:** The mouse cursor is hidden while the controller is in use, in both cursor modes. Shoulder buttons switch the tablet and Settings tabs; directional input stays inside the current page. In Supply, left/right moves between the available-item list and Clear/Place Order, with up/down moving through either column. Shelf stocking and unstocking repeat while their controller buttons are held, like mouse holds; the yellow shelf selection is drawn over the item artwork. Cash collection advances through the customer's money while the primary button is held, but change-drawer presses add exactly one bill or coin. Held actions stop on release, focus/screen changes, or loss of window focus, and never carry from cash collection into the change drawer.

## Part 2: Spatial Organization & Stock
* **Step 4: Item Data Contract:** Items possess `baseDimensions` (w, h), `isRotated` toggles, `supportedDisplayTypes`, and a `stack` value (how many units of stack depth on a display this item consumes). 
* **Step 47: Item Granularity (Tags):** Items use Unique Branded Assets (e.g., "Aulin NT Assault Rifle") categorized by combinative Tags (e.g., `[Weapon]`, `[Surplus]`).
* **Step 5: Fixture Data Contract:** Shelves feature `gridDimensions` and a `displayType`. Each shelf space holds one kind of item, up to 20 box-size units: an item's `boxSize` sets how many fit (a rifle with box size 4 fills a space at 5; a size 1 item fills it at 20). Stacked items show a quantity badge instead of rendering overlapping sprites. The shelf's placement/display grid is tile-derived: two uniform visual slots per floor tile in each direction. On the shop floor, shelves show no per-item icons; instead `ShelfAttentionHighlight` draws a pulsing green outline, following the shelf's footprint in every facing, around any shelf that has a space for an item in a crate the player is carrying (sold-out spaces included), so the player can see where their crates go. Shelves show no outline when the player carries nothing matching; low or sold-out stock alone no longer triggers it. The earlier icon-and-count badges and category-colored stock boxes were removed, because fixed icon layouts could not follow a turned shelf's shape. Inventory placement rules and saved stock remain unchanged.
* **Step 22: Hybrid Fixtures:** Universal shelves hold anything that fits physically; specialized displays (e.g., weapon racks) restrict items by Tag but offer massive space efficiency.
* **Step 41: Build Mode:** While the store is closed, use MOVE in the Manager Tablet's Fixtures or Layout tab to rearrange fixtures, or carry a fixture crate and press E to place it. Left-click confirms; right-click or Escape cancels. WASD/arrows walk normally while placing, and the camera follows. Fixtures move in 8-pixel increments on unlocked floor; down-facing shelves may tuck their backs halfway into the wall. Placement preserves entrances, the loading area, connected walkways, clear fixture fronts, and register access/queue space. Customers and staff follow smooth, collision-aware routes around fixtures, so an in-between placement blocks only the space their bodies cannot fit through. Every placeable fixture, including the trashcan and register, uses a Collision Box: a body box fitted to its visible art, plus a blue projection box attached across its front. Registers also reserve a rear projection box for the cashier. The body outline is green (red if invalid). These outlines follow the fixture's exact position and facing instead of snapping separately to floor squares. Projection boxes stay walkable, can share an aisle or the floor in front of a doorway, and cannot be covered by furniture; only body boxes are physical obstacles. Fixtures and their projections must still stay out of the doorway itself and the loading pad. Transparent art margins are excluded from both placement bounds and physical bodies. Visible wall trim meets the buildable floor grid, including along the two-tile back wall, so fixtures placed against it have no artificial gap.
* **Fixture Purchasing, Crating & Resale:** The Fixtures tab lists prices and required fixture licenses. Buying delivers a distinct fixture crate to the Loading Pad; the tab confirms the purchase in green and lists free Loading Pad spots and fixture crates waiting there. Empty fixtures can be crated only while the store is closed; the crate appears where the fixture stood. Fixture crates can be carried, dropped, saved, and placed, but cannot be stacked with item crates. While closed, empty placed fixtures and fixture crates can be sold for half their listed price after confirmation. A fixture containing stock and the only register cannot be sold; the only register cannot be crated either.
* **Step 63: Reorganizing Loaded Fixtures:** Fixtures move with their entire contents intact, without spawning crates. The original position remains unchanged until a valid placement is confirmed.
* **Fixture Paint:** Facing a shelf or register and holding F on the keyboard or RT on a controller opens a Change Color window for that one fixture, whenever the player can interact, including during a shift. Eleven colors are free for now: Purple (the default), Gray, Steel, Black, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Teal, Blue, and Pink. Budget Shelves are drawn from gray art and tinted; registers get a copy of their art where only the purple body is recolored, so their outline, lights, and screen keep their colors. A fixture keeps its color when moved, packed into a crate, placed again, saved, and loaded. Later, colors will be unlocked and possibly mixed.

## Part 3: Procurement & Logistics
* **Step 10: The Loading Pad & Abstracted Inventory:** Ordered stock spawns as physical crates on an outdoor Loading Pad. Item crates are color-coded by group: weapons (Firearm and Melee), ammunition, attachments, clothing (Apparel), armor, tools, and survival supplies. Players can carry up to three item crates from the start, with the newest on top; crates stay straight, sit flush, and cover the lids below. The player holds crates above their head with matching raised-arm standing and walking sprites in all four directions. Two crates slow the player, and three crates slow them further; the player still faces the direction they move. A green arrow marks the reachable crate the player is facing. Tapping Space picks up that crate; holding Space picks it up with every crate above it. Tapping Q puts the top carried crate down; holding Q sets the whole carried stack down. With the arrow on a floor crate, Q stacks onto it instead (tap one, hold as many as fit). Floor stacks go up to three crates high, never on a fixture crate or the Loading Pad, and fixture crates are never stacked. The player walks behind floor stacks, taller stacks are harder to push, and the player can never move more than four crates at once, counting pushed stacks and carried crates. Customers shove loose crates out of their way regardless of weight; when two customers push the same crates, the one further from their destination keeps pushing while the other steps aside and tries again a moment later. A crate wedged so it can't move is squeezed past. A bottom bar shows the marked crate's picture, name, and count, with one card per carried crate above it. The player can also deliver the top crate directly to a nearby shelf.
* **No Hovercart:** The hovercart was removed; all stock moves in carried crates. Older saves are refunded the price of any hovercart upgrades, and items left in an old hovercart (or on a removed shelf) go to the Go-Backs bin.
* **Step 62: Empty Crates:** Empty crates remain reusable. E at a sales-floor trashcan returns all held empty crates, including empties below filled crates; filled crates remain held. E only supplies one free empty crate when the player is holding nothing. Q retains its normal floor placement/stacking behavior beside the trashcan. A trashcan is a cheap fixture (Basic Fixture Supply contract) that can be moved, crated, or sold like any other, only on the sales floor. New shops start with one beside the front door; older saves can buy one.
* **Empty-crate carry load:** Filled and fixture crates retain the existing speed multipliers (two: 75%, three: 50%). Each empty crate contributes only a quarter of a filled crate's share of that slowdown, including mixed stacks. Two empty crates allow 150 pixels/second walking; three allow 140, compared with 120 and 80 for filled stacks. A single crate already permits full speed. Dashing uses the same relative slowdown.
* **Shelf-window item pictures:** Every assigned shelf space displays its item's picture centered over the boxes. The picture remains when sold out, identifying the reserved item, and disappears when that space is deliberately cleared.
* **Customer cornering and collision recovery:** Customers reach corner waypoints precisely before turning, with movement capped to avoid overshooting. Routes retain the safe connection from an off-grid starting point. When a customer collides in their intended direction, they immediately calculate a fresh obstacle-aware route to the same destination; retries are limited to one every 200 milliseconds, and do not reset travel timeouts. Debug captures record these collision reroutes.
* **Register keypad editing:** Backspace removes the last typed character, including a decimal point. CLEAR (and Delete) still clears the whole entry.
* **Step 29: Unified Market:** All unlocked items populate in a single, unified DOM catalog.
* **Step 45: Supplier Discovery (MVP):** All potential supplier contracts, including fixture licenses, are visible from Day 1 in the Tablet. Basic Rations & Water is the only supply contract available to sign at Shop Level 1; Frontier Survival Kit becomes available at Level 2, Scrap-Yard Sidearms and Standard Pistol Ammo at Level 3, and Scrap-Yard Melee at Level 5. Other supplier contracts unlock at their assigned Shop Levels, while faction reputation requirements still apply where specified. The starting fixture license covers Budget Shelves and the register; the other shelf models were removed. A new game starts with a register but no shelves, so the first shelf is bought from the Fixtures tab. The starting money and starting layout come from the Starting Template (`data/StartingTemplate.json`), which the DEV Starting Template area can rewrite.
* **Step 48: Contract-Driven Demand:** Customers only expect (and penalize you for missing) items from Supplier Contracts you have *actively signed and paid for*.

## Part 4: The Checkout Flow (Updated)
* **Purchase quantities:** Customers want 1-3 units of each chosen item (50% one, 35% two, 15% three), except items that fit fewer than 10 to a shelf space (box size 3 or more), which they buy one at a time. They fill that quantity across stocked shelf placements and shelves, subject to their existing price limit. Every unit is a separate cart entry with its price recorded when picked up, so it is scanned and charged once.
* **Cash hand-over input:** Opening the customer's cash screen clears the held scan gesture. The player must press the mouse button again before sweeping over bills and coins; holding the button from item scanning cannot collect cash.
* **Scanning motion:** Only the selected item slides rapidly across the scanner. Unscanned items keep their visible positions when the queue closes a gap, then continue at the normal belt speed of 260 pixels per second. Clicking one item never snaps the rest of the cart forward. Drag-scanning requires mouse movement with the button held; items moving beneath a stationary held cursor do not scan themselves.
* **Step 6: The Grab & Queue:** Customers snapshot an item's price into their virtual cart when grabbing it off the physical shelf. They then path to the register to wait in queue. The line is single file: the first customer stands right at the counter (as close as the player stands behind it). The line prefers to run straight out from the counter and turn toward the store door, but can take another connected turn when needed. In a tight shop, it can also turn beside the counter, but not behind it. Customers stand an even step apart along the line, using available standing space even when a fixture partly covers a floor square. The line may run past shelf fronts, so a checkout aisle lined with shelves (a "gauntlet") works: customers walk in through the aisle, queue in it, and leave back out the way they came, never behind the counter. Customers never walk directly through obstacles when no safe route exists; they skip unreachable shelves or leave if they cannot reach checkout. Customers come in through the left (entrance) door and leave through the right (exit) door, spreading out in front of it.
* **Step 53: Customer Reactions:** Customers in line display color-coded bubbles that escalate as their patience drains. Separate drawn face reactions appear for notable events: an item is too expensive and skipped, an outrageous price makes them leave, a wanted item is out of stock, the customer leaves angry, or they find a bargain. Floating messages name unaffordable or missing products; customers also explain when they cannot reach a stocked shelf. Only one face reaction is shown at a time; each event can trigger once per customer, and the face fades after about two seconds. Customers start with 40 seconds of patience, or 24 seconds if impatient, plus 10 seconds for each person ahead when they join the line. Moving up adds 5 seconds unless they reach the front, where their patience resets and no longer runs down, even if nobody is working the register. Temporarily closing the store pauses the waiting customers' patience.
* **Step 54: The Checkout Interface:** Pressing E at the register (a prompt shows **E: Activate Register** / **Q: Go-Backs** in range) steps the player behind the counter. The scan window opens only while a customer has items, and can't be closed until they're done; leaving the register is the only way out. Items ride a conveyor belt toward a red scanner bar on the right and queue against it. The player clicks (or drag-hovers) each one to "scan" it, triggering a beep as it slides over the scanner.
* **Step 55: Receiving Payment:** Store prices, sales, and earnings remain in Galactic Credits, with arithmetic in integer cents. About half of customers pay by card and enter the total on the keypad; others pay cash. About 75% of Scavengers use M-Scrips, with the rest using the usual credit card/cash methods. M-Scrips convert at 120 per credit; fractional credit totals round up to the next whole M-Scrip. M-Scrips are bills in denominations of 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 500, and 1,000. The amount due, cash tendered, and change drawer are all shown in M-Scrips, but the sale earns the original credit total. About 75% of Explorers use one travel Chit, with the rest using the usual card/cash methods. The Chit is a reusable programmable device with a textured octagonal plasteel housing, not paper. Its small screen shows its credit value and a visible 2-5 digit code made of digits 1-4. The player enters the code using arrow keys, the D-pad, or clickable keys mapped up=1, down=2, left=3, right=4. Each attempt at the code has a 10-second timer shown as a shrinking bar that turns amber then red; correct keys do not refill it, and it restarts only after a mistake. A wrong input or running out of time resets progress and counts as a mistake, and three mistakes lose the sale; the sale completes immediately after the final correct digit, and the customer takes back the Chit. No change is given for a Chit. For cash, once the last item is scanned, the customer holds out bills (and, for credits, coins) that the player takes by clicking, or by holding the mouse button and sweeping across them. About 1 in 4 cash customers pays the exact amount, and the sale completes once their money is taken. Otherwise the player counts change from an unlimited drawer. The shorter change-counting view gives CURRENT CHANGE a large amber amount on the left, distinct from the smaller CHANGE OWED amount on the right. Credit bills are 1, 5, 10, and 20 credits; coins are 1, 5, 10, 25, and 50 cents. Wrong change is refused: the first mistake per sale warns, the second costs 1 store reputation (not faction reputation), and the third loses the sale. The customer leaves angry and their items go to the Go-Backs bin, with no further reputation loss. The sale earns only the cart total, not the tendered amount.
* **Step 12: The Go-Backs Bin:** If a customer's patience hits zero, they leave angry and abandon their cart. These items teleport to an infinite-capacity "Go-Backs" bin at the register. Pressing Q at the register with empty hands packs the bin into crates, in as few floor stacks as possible, set down as close to the register as possible without blocking the queue, doors, or fixture access. Nothing can be put into the bin. While the bin holds anything, a yellow "!" floats above the player.

## Part 5: Shift Mechanics & Economics
* **Step 11: The Soft Close:** At hour 12, doors lock to new foot traffic, but the shift doesn't end until the register queue drops to zero.
* **Step 43: Finances Tab & End Day:** When the shift ends, reputation changes are applied and a five-second "Shop Closed" screen summarizes customers served and turned away, including with reduced-motion settings. Clicking "End Day" on the top bar immediately updates the calendar and market, without opening Finances or requiring another click. Itemized receipts remain available in Finances, which also shows the pending reputation change during a shift. Each end-of-day receipt counts customers who were pleased by a bargain, skipped items because of price, or left because prices were outrageous. A customer is counted once for each kind of price reaction they had. Wages are paid at the start of each shift. The button then becomes "Start Shift" for the next day.
* **Step 52: Market Fluctuation & Pricing:** Item market prices change only when the player clicks "End Day," allowing stress-free repricing during closed hours. Each customer's bargain threshold varies between 80% and 90% of market price; their item-skip threshold varies between 110% and 120%; and their disgust threshold varies between 150% and 170%. A bargain purchase makes the customer happy and gives one extra store-reputation point per visit, even if they buy several bargains. Price-based reputation changes never affect faction reputation. Above the skip threshold, the customer skips only that item and keeps shopping; above the disgust threshold, they leave immediately and lose store reputation. If they skip all main items because of price, they continue to their other listed items. A customer has a 50% chance to add up to two unplanned bargain items to their list. In Inventory, each item can be pinned to the market price so it automatically follows the market when it changes; other item prices remain unchanged until the player adjusts them. Inventory shows profit per item as the store price minus the supplier's crate price divided by the number of items in the crate, with the per-item cost rounded to the nearest cent. The separate delivery fee is not included.
* **Step 32 & 68: Bankruptcy & Wages:** Employee wages are paid upfront at the start of the shift, even if it dips funds into the red. If funds fall below zero due to automated deductions, players cannot make new purchases. Running out of sellable stock while negative results in an unrecoverable game-over soft-lock.

## Part 6: Employees & Automation
* **Step 19: The Hiring Pool:** All employees start identical (500 to hire, 100 in wages per shift, carrying 10 items). Shop Level 4 allows one hire, and Levels 7, 10, 15, 18, and 23 each allow one more at once. Each training course costs more than that employee's previous course, whichever skill it trains. Stock shelves adds 5 carried items per level, plus another 5 at Level 3. Staff can be let go between shifts, without a hiring-cost refund; skills and increased wages persist if they are hired again.
* **Employee Training:** Register, Stocking, and Cleaning each have Levels 1-5. Between shifts, the Employee tab lets the player choose a skill on each employee's card and preview the next level, speed, course price, wages, and paid absence before booking. A course takes the entire next shift, while the employee still earns their current wage. The chosen skill gains one level at shift end. A booked trainee is unavailable for their training shift and cannot be booked for another course or switched On Shift / Off Shift until they return. Each gained level adds 20% job speed; Register also gains 10% speed at Levels 3 and 5, reaching 1.5x normal speed at Level 3 and 2x at Level 5. Each course adds 10% of the original daily wage. Money increases are rounded up to whole credits. Skills and booked courses are saved. Training fees appear on the daily receipt.
* **On Shift / Off Shift:** Employees are never fired. Each hired employee is On Shift (works and is paid at shift start) or Off Shift (stays home, unpaid, keeps skills). Store Level caps how many can be hired; every hired employee may be On Shift. Trainees are away (and paid) for their training shift regardless of the setting.
* **Step 17 & 18: Scheduling & Clocking Out:** At 20:00, employees stop taking new stocking and cleaning jobs and finish their current task, including returning a partly used crate or disposing of an empty one. Register service for customers already inside remains their final task: they serve the remaining queue, including shoppers who reach it after closing. Once no register service remains, they immediately walk to the public front door using the normal paths and backroom portals, open it on approach, and disappear at the exit. The shift waits until all staff have physically left, not merely reached a breakroom.
* **Step 13 & 50: Task Priorities (MVP):** Each employee can run the register, stock shelves, and clean trash when the cleaning module is enabled. Drag the jobs into top-to-bottom order in the Employee tab: highest priority first. Move up / down buttons provide keyboard/controller alternatives. Changes are only allowed between shifts and the complete order is saved.
* **Step 14 & 15: Employee Logistics:** Hired staff can run the register or take item crates anywhere in the shop or backroom to matching shelf spots. Stocking transfers one item after each handling interval, rather than instantly unloading the crate. The provisional Level 1 interval is 1000ms per item; Levels 1–5 divide that interval by stocking speeds 1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, and 1.8. Fifteen items require fifteen intervals. A partly used crate is returned safely; empty crates go to a trashcan. This timing is provisional pending owner confirmation.
* **Step 16: Empty Stock Alerts:** If no matching item crate is available on the Loading Pad, the employee skips the shelf and pings the UI with an "Out of Stock" notification. They do not auto-purchase goods.
* **Step 65, 66 & 67: Break Room & Behavior:** When no assigned task is available during the shift, employees walk through the backroom door and wait out of sight in the backroom. As soon as a task opens, they step back out and resume work. At 20:00, staff on break or heading there return to serve customers still inside. They wait visibly on the sales floor if those shoppers have not yet reached a register. Busy staff finish their current task first, then help with remaining register service. They take no new stocking or cleaning jobs and walk out through the public front door immediately when no register service remains.

## Part 7: Factions, Customers, & Spawning
* **Step 24: Accumulative Spawning:** The total daily customer pool scales upward. Global Reputation dictates baseline traffic, while Faction reputations *add* their distinct members to the pool.
* **Step 49: Foot Traffic:** Baseline traffic acts as a direct multiplier of Global Store Reputation.
* **Step 51: Reputation Math:** New shops start at 0 store reputation. Successes and penalties are tallied throughout a shift, then the net change is applied once at shift end, before End Day. Faction customers affect both their faction and store reputation. Shops start at Level 1; Levels 2, 3, 4, and 5 need 20, 50, 90, and 138 total store reputation respectively. Serving every arriving customer without reputation penalties earns one level in each of the first four shifts; later reputation gaps require several shifts. Reputation gained per sale is unchanged. The provisional thresholds through Level 50 are in [StoreLevel.md](StoreLevel.md); earned levels remain even if reputation later falls, including levels earned on the older curve. Every level from 2 to 50 unlocks one or two things (supplier contracts, a higher staff limit, or a shop expansion); the full list and ordering rules are in [StoreLevel.md](StoreLevel.md).
* **Step 12: Intent + Impulse:** Customers build Primary and randomized Optional lists only from item types assigned to shelf slots (including sold-out slots), and visit relevant shelves that still have products. Primary lists grow by one item at each of the current first-pass store-reputation milestones (138, 1,128, and 2,343), in addition to the faction's starting list size; these milestones need review during balance testing. A missing Primary item costs one penalty total: -1 store reputation, plus -1 faction reputation for faction customers. They still buy available Primary items; they leave without buying if none of their Primary items are in stock. Missing Optional items have no penalty.
* **Step 25-28: The Factions:**
  * *Scavengers:* Buy low-tier/surplus. Larger Optional lists (impulse buyers). Most often pay with M-Scrips.
  * *Mercenaries:* Buy mid/high-tier Firearms, Ammunition, and Armor. Have a larger Primary list, seek matching ammunition for purchased firearms, and refuse duplicate weapon types.
  * *Explorers:* Seek Tools and Survival gear (either can fill their Primary list); may also buy Standard Firearms. Most often pay with travel Chits.
* **Customer Traits:** A customer's personality is built from traits (`js/logic/CustomerTraits.js`). Every customer has their group as a trait (Scavenger, Mercenary, or Explorer) and may also get personality traits:
  * *Wanderer (30% chance):* Besides the shelves holding what they want, also browses about half of the store's other stocked shelves, walking them in a random order. More stocked shelves means more wandering.
  * *Impatient (25% chance):* Starts with 40% less base patience before the same line-position bonuses as other customers. The patience bubble scales to their shorter fuse.
  * *Browser (30% chance, only in a well-stocked store):* Can only appear once the store has at least 4 shelves or at least 8 different items on its shelves. Adds 2 extra items to their might-also-buy list, picked from anything on the shelves, even outside their group's usual taste. They still won't pay above their price limit.
  * Traits aren't shown to the player. They exist to mix up encounters; new traits get added to the pool as we think of them.
  * Traits can have appearance conditions (like Browser's) so they only join the pool once the store meets them.

## Part 8: The Manager's Tablet & UI
* **Step 40: Tablet Tabs:** The central DOM UI features tabs for Inventory, Employee, Contract, Supply, Fixtures, Layout, Finances, and the Handbook.
* **Supply Tab Shelf Badges:** Items that already have a shelf spot get a colored badge and edge: red "SOLD OUT ON YOUR SHELVES", amber "RUNNING LOW" (under half full), or green "ON YOUR SHELVES". These sort to the top, most urgent first, so restocking is quick.
* **Tablet at the Register:** Opening the Manager's Tablet hides the checkout window without losing scanned items or payment progress. Arriving customers cannot open checkout over the Tablet. The shop keeps running, and customers behind the front of the line still lose patience. Closing the Tablet restores checkout for the front customer if the player is still operating the register; if that customer left for another reason, checkout starts for the next customer instead.
* **Step 36 & 46: Feedback & Manuals:** Contextual "God Windows" teach mechanics once. On-canvas feedback uses clean Emote Bubbles. The Employee Manual/Handbook acts as a permanent reference guide.
* **Step 44: MVP Onboarding:** Minimalist pre-shift tutorial via God Windows teaching the procurement-to-shelf loop (buy rifles, grab from pad, inventory, stock, set price, start shift).
* **Step 35: Screen Real Estate:** Absolute-positioned HTML windows float over a 100% full-screen canvas viewport.
* **Step 57: Camera Control:** The game uses a fixed 1920x1080 design frame that scales uniformly to fill the window without stretching; mismatched aspect ratios may leave black bars. The Phaser scene renders at its fixed design size, then a separate canvas resamples its finished image to the selected resolution before scaling it up to fill the frame. This softens the game image at lower choices without a pixelation effect; DOM windows remain sharp. It changes the visible image resolution, not Phaser's internal render workload or the monitor's display mode. Windowed mode resizes the window to the chosen resolution. Inside the frame, the game starts zoomed so the shop the player owns fills the screen. On the sales floor, PageUp zooms in and PageDown zooms out; zoom controls do nothing in the backroom or other rooms. Fully zoomed out, the whole owned shop always fits on one screen. When zoomed in past that, the camera follows the player. Buying an expansion keeps the current zoom, and the player can zoom out to see the bigger shop. The full shop, with every expansion, is capped at 39 by 21 tiles. Pop-ups (prompts, labels, customer bubbles, windows) stay the same size at any zoom.
* **Step 57a: Backroom Window:** The backroom (Storeroom and Loading Pad) is its own small map and takes no Sales Floor space. Pressing E in front of the backroom door in the Sales Floor's top wall opens a framed "BACKROOM" window over the middle of the screen, with the player inside it; the shop stays visible, dimmed, around it and keeps running, so customers keep shopping. Pressing E in front of the exit door in the backroom's top wall closes the window and puts the player back on the Sales Floor just inside the backroom door. Carried crates come along, and employees walk through the same doors. The starting backroom has four usable floor columns, including a walking aisle beside the unchanged loading pad, and an extra row of floor above the pad. This starting width is provisional. Backroom upgrades widen the room and its window without changing its height or moving the pad or exit. The old backroom space west of the Sales Floor becomes a sales floor expansion. The Loading Pad is drawn as a steel deck with a yellow-and-black hazard border and nine marked crate bays, matching the drone terminal's pad, with no text label.
* **Modal Windows & Anchors:** UI windows act as strict modals (clicking outside does not close them). Closing requires an `Esc` key or a dedicated close button. Anchor-based movement relies on clicking an item, shelf, crate, or register; character pathfinding triggers the relevant UI *only upon arrival*, and anchor-clicking is disabled while any window is open.

## Part 9: Visuals, Physics & Save Data
* **Step 30 & 58: Player Collision & Input:** No dynamic entity collision (pawns glide through each other). The player avatar operates at the highest Z-index (9999) to remain visible.
* **Player Art:** The player uses the blue-jacket mechanic's four-direction sprite sheet, with walking frames while moving, a faster cycle while dashing, and a standing frame when stopped or operating the register. The art retains its original colors and uses nearest-neighbor filtering. The lower-body collision box remains 22 by 20 world pixels.
* **Customer Art:** Customers and employees use animated four-direction walking characters, with a standing pose when stopped, browsing, waiting in line, or paused. A character's look tells you their faction: armored and security types are Mercenaries, adventurers and travelers are Explorers, and mutants and scrappy cyborgs are Scavengers; civilians and androids belong to no faction. Employees use the same characters with a green tint. Each character's collision box stays 46 by 48 world pixels, so lines and register reach are unchanged.
* **Step 38: Aesthetics:** Clean, flat-shaded 2D pixel-art characters with simple three-frame walk cycles in four directions to keep animation logic lean.
* **Step 37: Audio:** Dynamic crossfading music tied directly to the Shift State (Pre-Shift, Active Shift, Post-Shift).
* **Step 34: Data Persistence:** Save Game creates or overwrites named regular saves only when the player saves manually, between shifts. Choosing a card asks before replacing its progress; Create New Save asks for a save file name. Right-clicking a save asks before deleting it. Deleting the save being played does not leave the shop; the player can keep playing and save again before closing. Clicking End Day on the receipt advances the day and writes an autosave before dismissing the receipt. Autosaves have three reserved slots shared across shops; once full, the oldest by save time is overwritten. They never change regular saves and appear only in Load Game/Continue, not Save Game. Loading an autosave does not select it as a manual-save destination. A failed receipt autosave keeps the receipt open with a retry button; retry does not advance the day again. There are no automatic writes on new-game creation, during preparation, or before starting a shift. Save metadata captures the date/time and total play time at saving; play time includes preparation and management but excludes the pause menu. Loading restores a closed shop at the public entrance, including fixture positions/contents, floor/carried crates, money, reputation, Shop Level, licenses, store prices, market day/prices/supplier rates, and unlocked rooms. Mid-shift exits resume whichever saved snapshot the player loads, not active customers. Electron saves the library atomically to `save-slots.json` in its user-data directory; browser play uses `space-arms-saves-v2` in localStorage (separate saves). The former single-save files are not imported. Save/load failures are shown explicitly.
