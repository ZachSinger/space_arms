# Space Arms Dealer Simulation - Master Technical & Design Blueprint (Updated Steps 1-68)

## Current Stretch Goals List (Post-MVP)
* **Active Theft / Security Mini-Game:** Physical interception of shoplifters on the canvas.
* **The Military Faction:** A fourth customer demographic with unique bulk/high-tier purchasing habits.
* **Faction-Specific Rewards:** Tangible bonuses or unlocks for maximizing reputation with specific groups.
* **Alternative Bankruptcy / Bailout Systems:** Debt spirals or faction loans to prevent a soft-lock game over.
* **Advanced Supplier Discovery System:** Actively researching/discovering new suppliers beyond the default list.

---

## Part 1: Core Architecture & Time
* **Step 1: Platform & Tech Stack:** PC Desktop (Electron using Vite, vanilla HTML/CSS/JS frontend, and Phaser 3 canvas). The UI and Canvas are strictly decoupled.
* **Step 2: Time Management:** Real-time simulation using Galactic Time Code (GTC) with a "Manual Toggle". Shifts reach soft close after 12 GTC hours at a **Static Speed**, or 5 minutes of unpaused play; time pauses entirely when closed, allowing infinite downtime for management.
* **Step 3: The MVP Bridge:** Powered by a Master State (single source of truth) and an Event Bus to handle communication between the "dumb" UI and the Canvas.
* **Player Dash & Stamina:** Hold Shift while moving with WASD/arrows to dash at 280 pixels per second instead of walking at 160. Only the player has this ability; employees are unchanged. The HUD shows a stamina bar below the status bar. Starting balance: 100 stamina, 25 used per second of dashing, and 25 recovered per second while walking or resting after a 0.75-second delay. At zero stamina the player walks until stamina recovers to 25, preventing rapid speed changes at an empty bar. Shift pause freezes stamina along with movement; management windows allow recovery while the player rests. Stamina starts full when a game is loaded and is not saved.
* **Controller Support:** Any standard controller (Xbox layout names) can play the whole game; prompts switch to controller buttons as soon as it's used and back to keys on any real key press or mouse use. In the shop: left stick or D-pad walks, either trigger (or clicking the left stick) dashes, A interacts (same as E), X picks up/drops/throws crates (Space), Y opens Go-Backs at the register (Q), B leaves the register, Back/View opens the Manager's Tablet, Start opens the top bar (pause, start shift, end day, save, close store, main menu), and LB/RB zoom. In any window, the D-pad or left stick moves a yellow highlight; A clicks, X acts as right-click, B goes back (from the register screen it leaves the register), LB/RB switch tablet tabs, and the right stick scrolls. On a shelf, Y picks up the highlighted stack to move it, or turns the item being placed. When moving a fixture, the D-pad steps it one tile, Y turns it, A places it, and B cancels.

## Part 2: Spatial Organization & Stock
* **Step 4: Item Data Contract:** Items possess `baseDimensions` (w, h), `isRotated` toggles, `supportedDisplayTypes`, and a `stack` value (how many units of stack depth on a display this item consumes). 
* **Step 47: Item Granularity (Tags):** Items use Unique Branded Assets (e.g., "Aulin NT Assault Rifle") categorized by combinative Tags (e.g., `[Weapon]`, `[Surplus]`).
* **Step 5: Fixture Data Contract:** Shelves feature `gridDimensions`, a `displayType`, and a `stackDepth` capacity (the most units a single slot on this fixture can hold). Each item's `stackSize` is how many of that item fit in one slot (max 10), capped by the shelf's `stackDepth`. For example, a Rifle with a `stackSize` of 3 fills a slot at 3; a paste tube with a `stackSize` of 10 fills it at 10. Stacked items show an "xN" badge instead of rendering overlapping sprites. The shelf's placement/display grid is tile-derived: two uniform visual slots per floor tile in each direction. Each stocked placement is drawn in-world as one mostly opaque dark block with a category-colored outline, spanning the item's existing `dimensions`, with an `xN` quantity label; do not draw one sprite per unit.
* **Step 22: Hybrid Fixtures:** Universal shelves hold anything that fits physically; specialized displays (e.g., weapon racks) restrict items by Tag but offer massive space efficiency.
* **Step 41: Build Mode:** While the store is closed, use MOVE in the Manager Tablet's Fixtures or Layout tab to rearrange fixtures, or carry a fixture crate and press E to place it. Left-click confirms; right-click or Escape cancels. WASD/arrows pan during placement. Fixtures snap to unlocked floor; placement preserves entrances, the loading area, connected walkways, clear fixture fronts, and register access/queue space. Visible wall trim meets the buildable floor grid, including along the two-tile back wall, so fixtures placed against it have no artificial gap.
* **Fixture Purchasing, Crating & Resale:** The Fixtures tab lists prices and required fixture licenses. Buying delivers a distinct fixture crate to the Loading Pad; the tab confirms the purchase in green and lists free Loading Pad spots and fixture crates waiting there. Empty fixtures can be crated only while the store is closed; the crate appears where the fixture stood. Fixture crates can be carried, dropped, thrown, saved, and placed, but cannot be stocked in storage. While closed, empty placed fixtures and fixture crates can be sold for half their listed price after confirmation. A fixture containing stock and the only register cannot be sold; the only register cannot be crated either.
* **Step 63: Reorganizing Loaded Fixtures:** Fixtures move with their entire contents intact, without using hovercart capacity or spawning crates. The original position remains unchanged until a valid placement is confirmed.

## Part 3: Procurement & Logistics
* **Step 10: The Loading Pad & Abstracted Inventory:** Ordered stock spawns as physical crates on an outdoor Loading Pad. Crates must be hauled inside via chained anchor pathfinding. 
* **Hovercart (Backpack) Abstracted Model:** The hovercart is an abstract character inventory size rather than a physical moving entity on the map. New games start with 15 spaces; some items take more than one space. The Equipment tab sells upgrades to 20, 25, 30, 35, and 40 spaces for 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 credits respectively, with no license required. The maximum is 40 spaces. Existing 20-space or larger carts keep their size and contents; older 10-space carts load at 15 spaces.
* **Step 62: Empty Crates:** Utilizes the "Abstracted Box" approach for the MVP—the moment the last item is removed from a crate into inventory, the empty crate instantly despawns.
* **Step 42: Backroom Storage:** Item crates can be dumped on the floor (clogging pathing) or placed on Storage Fixtures, which share the inventory array logic for easy drag-and-drop management. Fixture crates cannot be stocked in Storage Fixtures.
* **Step 29: Unified Market:** All unlocked items populate in a single, unified DOM catalog.
* **Step 45: Supplier Discovery (MVP):** All potential supplier contracts, including fixture licenses, are visible from Day 1 in the Tablet. Civilian Threads unlocks at Shop Level 2 and Expanded Fixture Supply at Level 3; other contracts retain their current store and faction reputation requirements until assigned to levels. The starting fixture license covers the budget shelf (one tile with 2x2 slots, the cheapest fixture), compact shelf, locker, and register types; the large universal shelf needs the Expanded Fixture Supply contract. A new game starts with two lockers and a register but no shelves, so the first shelf is bought from the Fixtures tab. Existing saves keep their lockers and signed contracts.
* **Step 48: Contract-Driven Demand:** Customers only expect (and penalize you for missing) items from Supplier Contracts you have *actively signed and paid for*.

## Part 4: The Checkout Flow (Updated)
* **Purchase quantities:** Customers want 1-3 units of each chosen item (50% one, 35% two, 15% three). They fill that quantity across stocked shelf placements and shelves, subject to their existing price limit. Every unit is a separate cart entry with its price recorded when picked up, so it is scanned and charged once.
* **Cash hand-over input:** Opening the customer's cash screen clears the held scan gesture. The player must press the mouse button again before sweeping over bills and coins; holding the button from item scanning cannot collect cash.
* **Step 6: The Grab & Queue:** Customers snapshot an item's price into their virtual cart when grabbing it off the physical shelf. They then path to the register to wait in queue. The line is single file: the first customer stands right at the counter (as close as the player stands behind it), the line runs straight out from the counter (the way the player faces when standing behind it) until a wall or fixture blocks it, then turns toward the store door. Customers stand an even step apart along the line. The line may run past shelf fronts, so a checkout aisle lined with shelves (a "gauntlet") works: customers walk in through the aisle, queue in it, and leave back out the way they came, never behind the counter. Customers never walk directly through obstacles when no safe route exists; they skip unreachable shelves or leave if they cannot reach checkout, and departing customers spread across the entrance.
* **Step 53: Customer Reactions:** Customers in line display color-coded bubbles that escalate as their patience drains. Separate drawn face reactions appear only for notable events: an item is too expensive, a wanted item is out of stock, the customer leaves angry, a customer completes a purchase of at least three items, or they find a bargain. A bargain is an item priced at least 10% below its market price. Floating messages name unaffordable or missing products; customers also explain when they cannot reach a stocked shelf or when their faction has no matching products available. Only one face reaction is shown at a time; each event can trigger once per customer, and the face fades after about two seconds. Ringing up *any* customer globally resets the patience timer for the next person in line.
* **Step 54: The Checkout Interface:** Pressing E at the register (a prompt shows **E: Activate Register** / **Q: Go-Backs** in range) steps the player behind the counter. The scan window opens only while a customer has items, and can't be closed until they're done; leaving the register is the only way out. Items ride a conveyor belt toward a red scanner bar on the right and queue against it. The player clicks (or drag-hovers) each one to "scan" it, triggering a beep as it slides over the scanner.
* **Step 55: Receiving Payment:** Multiple lore currencies exist (M-Scrips, Vouchers), but checkout amounts and change use Galactic Credits (100 cents per credit). Market and store prices support cents, and the keypad accepts decimals. About half of register customers pay by card and enter the total on the keypad; the rest pay cash. Once a cash customer's last item is scanned, the screen blurs and the customer holds out their bills and coins, scattered in the middle of the screen. The player takes each piece by clicking it, or by holding the mouse button and sweeping across them like scanning items. About 1 in 4 cash customers pays the exact amount (coins included), and the sale finishes as soon as their money is taken. Otherwise, the total line shows the sale total followed by the amount paid in green (for example, "TOTAL: 150.10 - 160.00"), and the player counts the displayed change owed from an unlimited drawer. Bills are 1, 5, 10, and 20 credits; coins are 1, 5, 10, 25, and 50 cents. Drawer bills and coins show only their number, and the selected pile is labeled "Current Change". Click adds a denomination to the change, right-click removes one, and Space hands over the selected change. Wrong change is refused: the first mistake per sale warns, and each later mistake costs 1 store reputation (not faction reputation). The sale earns only its total, not the cash tendered. Bills and coins use temporary drawn shapes.
* **Step 12: The Go-Backs Bin:** If a customer's patience hits zero, they leave angry and abandon their cart. These items teleport to an infinite-capacity "Go-Backs" bin, opened as its own window from the register UI. Items leave the bin only by being loaded into the hovercart, then restocked manually.

## Part 5: Shift Mechanics & Economics
* **Step 11: The Soft Close:** At hour 12, doors lock to new foot traffic, but the shift doesn't end until the register queue drops to zero.
* **Step 43: Finances Tab & End Day:** When the shift ends, reputation changes are applied and a five-second "Shop Closed" screen summarizes customers served and turned away, including with reduced-motion settings. The player can then open the itemized receipt. The Finances Tab shows the pending reputation change during a shift. Clicking "End Day" updates the calendar and market; wages are paid at the start of each shift. The button then becomes "Start Shift" for the next day.
* **Step 52: Market Fluctuation & Pricing:** Item market prices scramble dynamically *only* when the player clicks "End Day," allowing stress-free repricing during closed hours. Customers spawn with randomized price tolerance multipliers and will reject items if the markup exceeds their threshold. If an item is priced at least 10% below market, customers have a 50% chance to add one discounted item they did not already plan to buy; bargain purchases trigger a happy face but grant no extra reputation.
* **Step 32 & 68: Bankruptcy & Wages:** Employee wages are paid upfront at the start of the shift, even if it dips funds into the red. If funds fall below zero due to automated deductions, players cannot make new purchases. Running out of sellable stock while negative results in an unrecoverable game-over soft-lock.

## Part 6: Employees & Automation
* **Step 19: The Hiring Pool:** Tavi can initially be hired between shifts after reaching Shop Level 4, for 250 credits plus 50 credits in wages per shift. Other employees unlock at their listed Shop Levels. Staff can be let go between shifts, without a hiring-cost refund; skills and increased wages persist if they are hired again.
* **Employee Training:** Run Register, Stock Shelves, and Load Storage each have Levels 1-5. Pay for a course between shifts; the employee spends the entire next shift away, while still earning their current wage. The chosen skill gains one level at shift end. A booked trainee cannot be fired or booked for another course until they return. Starting balance: a course costs half the employee's original hiring price times the target level; each gained level adds 20% of normal speed for that job (register service, stocking-trip movement, or delivery-crate-trip movement), 10% of the original daily wage, and 25% of the original hiring price. Money increases are rounded up to whole credits. Skills and booked courses are saved; skills persist after firing. Training fees appear on the daily receipt.
* **Step 17 & 18: Scheduling & Clocking Out:** Employees are budgeted for 3 to 12-hour shifts. When their hours end, they politely finish their immediate micro-interaction before pathing off the canvas.
* **Step 13 & 50: Task Priorities (MVP):** Employees evaluate a top-to-bottom customizable task list: *Work Register*, *Stock Shelves*, and *Load Storage*. Every employee can do all three jobs; their saved priority order decides which available job they take first.
* **Step 14 & 15: Employee Logistics:** Hired staff utilize their own independent inventory capacity ("backpack") to move locker stock to shelf slots. For *Load Storage*, they move delivered item crates from the Loading Pad into lockers.
* **Step 16: Empty Stock Alerts:** If backroom stock is missing, the AI skips the shelf and pings the UI with an "Out of Stock" notification. They do not auto-purchase goods.
* **Step 65, 66 & 67: Break Room & Behavior:** Employees walk in like customers at shift start. When no assigned task is available, they walk through the backroom door and wait out of sight in the backroom. As soon as a task opens, they step back out and resume work. After closing starts, employees on break or heading there still return to the register for customers remaining in the store. Otherwise, employees finish walking into the backroom and count as clocked out; anyone carrying stock must return it before the shift can end.

## Part 7: Factions, Customers, & Spawning
* **Step 24: Accumulative Spawning:** The total daily customer pool scales upward. Global Reputation dictates baseline traffic, while Faction reputations *add* their distinct members to the pool.
* **Step 49: Foot Traffic:** Baseline traffic acts as a direct multiplier of Global Store Reputation.
* **Step 51: Reputation Math:** New shops start at 0 store reputation. Successes and penalties are tallied throughout a shift, then the net change is applied once at shift end, before End Day. Faction customers affect both their faction and store reputation. Shops start at Level 1; Levels 2, 3, and 4 need 15, 35, and 60 total store reputation respectively. The provisional thresholds through Level 30 are in [StoreLevel.md](StoreLevel.md); earned levels remain even if reputation later falls. Level 5-30 rewards are planned, not implemented.
* **Step 12: Intent + Impulse:** Customers build Primary and randomized Optional lists only from item types assigned to shelf slots (including sold-out slots), and visit relevant shelves that still have products. A missing Primary item costs one penalty total: -1 store reputation, plus -1 faction reputation for faction customers. They still buy available Primary items; they leave without buying if none of their Primary items are in stock. Missing Optional items have no penalty. List sizes are currently small and fixed by faction while reputation thresholds await playtesting.
* **Step 25-28: The Factions:**
  * *Scavengers:* Buy low-tier/surplus. Larger Optional lists (impulse buyers). Use M-Scrips.
  * *Mercenaries:* Buy mid/high-tier Firearms, Ammunition, and Armor. Have a larger Primary list, seek matching ammunition for purchased firearms, and refuse duplicate weapon types.
  * *Explorers:* Seek Tools and Survival gear (either can fill their Primary list); may also buy Standard Firearms. Use Vouchers.
* **Customer Traits:** A customer's personality is built from traits (`js/logic/CustomerTraits.js`). Every customer has their group as a trait (Scavenger, Mercenary, or Explorer) and may also get personality traits:
  * *Wanderer (30% chance):* Besides the shelves holding what they want, also browses about half of the store's other stocked shelves, walking them in a random order. More stocked shelves means more wandering.
  * *Impatient (25% chance):* Waits in line 40% less time before leaving angry. The patience bubble scales to their shorter fuse.
  * *Browser (30% chance, only in a well-stocked store):* Can only appear once the store has at least 4 shelves or at least 8 different items on its shelves. Adds 2 extra items to their might-also-buy list, picked from anything on the shelves, even outside their group's usual taste. They still won't pay above their price limit.
  * Traits aren't shown to the player. They exist to mix up encounters; new traits get added to the pool as we think of them.
  * Traits can have appearance conditions (like Browser's) so they only join the pool once the store meets them.

## Part 8: The Manager's Tablet & UI
* **Step 40: Tablet Tabs:** The central DOM UI features tabs for Inventory, Employee, Contract, Supply, Fixtures, Equipment, Layout, Finances, and the Handbook.
* **Supply Tab Shelf Badges:** Items that already have a shelf spot get a colored badge and edge: red "SOLD OUT ON YOUR SHELVES", amber "RUNNING LOW" (under half full), or green "ON YOUR SHELVES". These sort to the top, most urgent first, so restocking is quick.
* **Tablet at the Register:** Opening the Manager's Tablet hides the checkout window without losing scanned items or payment progress. Arriving customers cannot open checkout over the Tablet. The shop keeps running and waiting customers still lose patience. Closing the Tablet restores checkout for the front customer if the player is still operating the register; if that customer left, checkout starts for the next customer instead.
* **Step 36 & 46: Feedback & Manuals:** Contextual "God Windows" teach mechanics once. On-canvas feedback uses clean Emote Bubbles. The Employee Manual/Handbook acts as a permanent reference guide.
* **Step 44: MVP Onboarding:** Minimalist pre-shift tutorial via God Windows teaching the procurement-to-shelf loop (buy rifles, grab from pad, inventory, stock, set price, start shift).
* **Step 35: Screen Real Estate:** Absolute-positioned HTML windows float over a 100% full-screen canvas viewport.
* **Step 57: Camera Control:** The game starts zoomed so the shop the player owns fills the screen. PageUp zooms in and PageDown zooms out; fully zoomed out, the whole owned shop always fits on one screen at any window size or resolution. When zoomed in past that, the camera follows the player. Buying an expansion keeps the current zoom, and the player can zoom out to see the bigger shop. The full shop, with every expansion, is capped at 39 by 21 tiles. Pop-ups (prompts, labels, customer bubbles, windows) stay the same size at any zoom.
* **Step 57a: Backroom Window:** The backroom (Storeroom and Loading Pad) is its own small map and takes no Sales Floor space. Pressing E in front of the backroom door in the Sales Floor's top wall opens a framed "BACKROOM" window over the middle of the screen, with the player inside it; the shop stays visible, dimmed, around it and keeps running, so customers keep shopping. Pressing E in front of the exit door in the backroom's top wall closes the window and puts the player back on the Sales Floor just inside the backroom door. Carried crates come along, and employees walk through the same doors. Backroom upgrades make the backroom, and its window, bigger. The old backroom space west of the Sales Floor becomes a sales floor expansion.
* **Modal Windows & Anchors:** UI windows act as strict modals (clicking outside does not close them). Closing requires an `Esc` key or a dedicated close button. Anchor-based movement relies on clicking an item, shelf, crate, or register; character pathfinding triggers the relevant UI *only upon arrival*, and anchor-clicking is disabled while any window is open.

## Part 9: Visuals, Physics & Save Data
* **Step 30 & 58: Player Collision & Input:** No dynamic entity collision (pawns glide through each other). The player avatar operates at the highest Z-index (9999) to remain visible.
* **Step 38: Aesthetics:** Clean, flat-shaded 2D RimWorld-style vector pawns with no complex skeletal walk cycles to ensure lean animation logic.
* **Step 37: Audio:** Dynamic crossfading music tied directly to the Shift State (Pre-Shift, Active Shift, Post-Shift).
* **Step 34: Data Persistence:** Autosaving runs only off-shift and checkpoints before opening a shift. Continue restores a closed shop at the public entrance, including fixture positions/contents, hovercart, floor/carried crates, credits, reputation, Shop Level, licenses, store prices, market day/prices/supplier rates, and unlocked rooms. Older saves keep their reputation and hires; their initial Shop Level is based on their saved store reputation. Mid-shift exits resume the last off-shift checkpoint, not active customers. Electron saves atomically to `savegame.json` in its user-data directory; browser play uses localStorage (separate saves). Save/load failures are shown explicitly, and New Game requires confirmation before replacing a save.
